01 // Overview

The research

This project looked at the ethics of loot boxes in modern games: how close they are to gambling, and what they can do to younger and vulnerable players.

The literature review covered academic and industry discussion of gambling-like mechanics, psychological risk, player vulnerability, and regulation. It set out the main concerns with randomised rewards, especially when they involve real money, unclear odds, and persuasive design.

The research portfolio built on that with a qualitative analysis of modern games with loot box mechanics: how they're presented, accessed, and monetised. It looked at reward randomness, rarity, visual feedback, how open the odds are, how clear the pricing is, and whether it all feels like gambling.

It sharpened how I think about monetisation and ethical design, and how to connect industry debates to real examples.
